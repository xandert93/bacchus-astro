# Booking terms and conditions: draft

A full draft of the terms printed on every quote and accepted with the
deposit, for Bacchus to settle and a Maltese lawyer to check before use. It
keeps everything in the terms page of Bacchus's current wedding catalogue
(`public/docs/bacchus-wedding-catalogue.pdf`, pages 52–53), reworded in plain
English, and adds what venue terms normally cover that it doesn't.

**Status: draft, nothing here is settled.** Square brackets mark a value or a
choice that is Bacchus's to make; the decisions are listed first, numbered,
and each bracket names its decision (`[D4]`). Two of them are the open
conflicts in `client-facts.md`, so this draft must not be published or sent
to a client until they're settled.

**Not legal advice.** Terms with consumers (couples) must be fair under EU and
Maltese consumer law, which can void a clause that is too one-sided, however
clearly written. The notes on the decisions say where that matters most.

## Decisions for Bacchus

Each is needed before the terms can be used. Our suggestion follows each
question; it's a starting point, not a recommendation of law.

**The business**

- **D1. Who the contract is with.** The company is Bacchus Company Limited
  (from your quote). Still needed: its company number, registered address
  and VAT number.

**Money** (two of these are the open conflicts)

- **D2. The deposit.** The catalogue says 30%, non-refundable, worked out on
  the minimum guaranteed number of guests. Confirm.
- **D3. The payment schedule.** The catalogue says the remaining 70% on "an
  agreed payment structure", the last payment 14 days before the event. In
  person we heard 30/60/10 (the 60% over the following months, the last 10%
  a month before). Which, and with what dates? Suggestion: fixed dates on
  every quote, with couples free to pay more, earlier, at any time.
- **D4. Cancellation charges after the deposit.** The catalogue only says
  the deposit isn't refunded. What happens to later payments? Suggestion, a
  sliding scale (what you keep of everything paid, deposit included, by how
  long before the date the cancellation arrives):

  | Notice before the event | Bacchus keeps            |
  | ----------------------- | ------------------------ |
  | More than 12 months     | The deposit only         |
  | 6 to 12 months          | [50]% of the total price |
  | 3 to 6 months           | [75]% of the total price |
  | Less than 3 months      | 100% of the total price  |

  The law expects these to reflect the loss you'd really suffer (the less
  time to rebook the date, the bigger the loss), not a penalty. A scale like
  this is the usual way to show that.

- **D5. How long a quote stays valid.** The catalogue says 15 days; your
  quotes say 30. Suggestion: 30, as you use today.
- **D6. Price changes for events far ahead.** The catalogue lets you revise
  prices after a quote expires to reflect cost increases "at the time of the
  Event". For a confirmed booking, consumer law allows a price rise only if
  the terms say how it's worked out and let the couple cancel if it's large.
  Suggestion: prices are fixed once the deposit is paid, except a change in
  the VAT rate; for events more than [18] months away, one review [12]
  months before the date of no more than [the change in Malta's inflation
  rate], and if the rise is more than [5]%, the couple may cancel and get
  everything back except the deposit.
- **D7. Late payment.** How long after a missed payment before you may
  cancel? Suggestion: a written reminder, then [14] days to pay, then you
  may treat the booking as cancelled by the client (clause 15 applies).
- **D8. Card and payment fees.** Do you pass on card fees? Suggestion: no
  fee for bank payments; card payments [at no extra charge]. EU law bars
  surcharges on most consumer card payments, so the simplest answer is to
  absorb them and nudge towards bank payments.
- **D9. A damage deposit.** Do you want a refundable security deposit for
  damage? Suggestion: no; rely on clause 13.

**Guests and food**

- **D10. Minimum numbers.** Is there a minimum guaranteed number of guests
  per booking? The emails say there's no minimum; the catalogue works the
  deposit out on "the minimum guaranteed number". Suggestion: the couple
  names a minimum guaranteed number when booking, and that's what the
  deposit is calculated on.
- **D11. When final numbers, menu choices, dietary lists and the seating
  plan are due.** The catalogue says final numbers with the final payment,
  14 days before. Suggestion: menu choices [6] weeks before; final numbers,
  dietary list and seating plan [14] days before.
- **D12. Children.** Prices for children (by age), and is there a
  children's menu? Babies and toddlers free?
- **D13. Suppliers' meals.** Do you feed the photographer, band and so on,
  and at what price?
- **D14. Tastings.** The emails say about €40 a person, deducted if the
  couple books, free once they've paid; 16 items from their package; no
  stations. How many people may come, and when exactly does it become free
  (deposit paid, contract signed or paid in full)?
- **D15. Corkage.** €6.50 per bottle or per person? Which drinks may
  couples bring?
- **D16. Your own cake and outside food.** Couples may bring their own cake.
  Any charge for serving it? Any other outside food (sweets, favours, a
  cultural dish)?

**The day**

- **D17. Hours.** Packages are usually 5 hours, with overtime at about
  €10–15 a person per extra hour (emails). Confirm the overtime rate, the
  latest finishing time, and the access times for setting up and clearing
  away.
- **D18. Music.** Outdoor music until 23:00 and indoor until 04:00
  (emails). Confirm, and any sound limit.
- **D19. Supplier access to Mdina.** The catalogue gives 06:00–10:30 and
  15:30–17:00 without a permit, a Local Council permit otherwise. Still
  right?
- **D20. Parking.** What is "the Tomba", and what can guests book?
- **D21. What's not allowed.** Confetti (which kinds, where), candles,
  sparklers, smoke and haze machines, fireworks, sky lanterns, shisha (the
  emails suggest the Secret Garden as a shisha area). Suggestions are in
  clause 11.
- **D22. Exclusive use.** When is the venue exclusive to one event, and
  when may other guests be on the estate? The emails say the downstairs
  restaurant closes to the public above 150 guests.
- **D23. Lost property.** How long do you keep things left behind?
  Suggestion: [30] days.
- **D24. Cloakroom and security.** The catalogue offers a cloakroom with an
  attendant at a charge. Is security staff ever required (large events)?

**Changes and the unforeseen**

- **D25. Moving the date.** If a couple wants a different date: suggestion,
  one move, free if asked more than [12] months before the original date
  and the new date is within [12] months of it, at the new date's prices;
  otherwise treated as a cancellation and a new booking.
- **D26. Events beyond anyone's control** (a pandemic, a government ban, a
  natural disaster). The catalogue only says you're not liable. What
  happens to the couple's money? Suggestion: first, a free move to any
  available date within [18] months; if none suits, a refund of everything
  paid except costs you can show were already spent on their event. Keeping
  the whole deposit for an event that couldn't happen through nobody's fault
  is the kind of term consumer law most often strikes out.
- **D27. Complaints.** How long after the event may a complaint be made,
  and who handles it? Suggestion: in writing within [14] days; a reply
  within [14] days.

**Signing**

- **D28. Accepting the terms.** The catalogue says a booking is confirmed
  once the deposit is paid "and all necessary documentation signed", and
  your quote speaks of the contract being signed. Is a separate signed
  contract needed, or does accepting these terms on the deposit page (a
  ticked box, recorded with the date and wording) do? The second is just
  as binding and is what the site is built for.
